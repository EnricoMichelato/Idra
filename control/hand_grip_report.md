# Hand grip force (estimate)

Generated by `control/hand_grip.py`. Motor: 25 mm metal gearmotor, 12 V, 75:1 (0.83 Nm stall at 1.8 A, 100 rpm). Worm: steel, module 1, PD 16, single start, right hand. Wheels: 28 teeth, printed.

## Force against current limit

**Chosen: 0.75 A (R_ILIM 82 kΩ).** About 16 N per finger, 1.0 kg held by friction alone with a safety factor of 2 and 5 m/s² of arm acceleration. The Fin Ray fingers also wrap the object, which adds to this.

## Other numbers

- **Worm axial load:** 188 N, pushing the worm **up**, away from the motor. It goes into the F6-12M thrust ball bearing of the palm dome, not into the gearmotor.
- **Worm efficiency:** 29%. The lead angle is 3.6°, so the worm cannot be back-driven for any friction above 0.06; the 75:1 gearbox adds to that. The grip holds with the power off.
- **Speed:** 100 rpm at the worm, 21°/s at the fingers. Fully open to an 80 mm object: 1.6 s. Fully open to tips touching: 3.2 s (5.4 worm turns).
- **Power through the coils:** about 9 W for the fraction of a second at stall, 2–4 W while closing. The link has to deliver this: check it on the bench with a 15 Ω load.
- **Wheel teeth:** 18 MPa at stall on a module 1 printed tooth. Print the finger roots flat, 100% infill around the teeth; PA-CF is better than PETG here.
- **Gearmotor:** it works at 39% of its stall torque, only while squeezing. The close command turns the bridge off 30 ms after the stall is detected.

## Assumptions

- Contact 55 mm from the finger pivot; TPU friction 0.6; the three fingers share the load equally.
- Gearmotor torque is proportional to current above the no-load current; stall figures are the maker's extrapolations.
- A wet or oily object halves the friction: the form closure of the Fin Rays then does most of the work.
