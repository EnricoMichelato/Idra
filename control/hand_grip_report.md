# Hand grip force (estimate)

Generated by `control/hand_grip.py`. Hand v3.0: Feetech STS3215 12 V servo (2.94 Nm stall at 2.7 A, 45 rpm) in motor mode. Worm: steel, module 1.5, PD 20, single start. Wheels: 18-tooth sectors, printed PA-CF.

## Force against the servo current (torque limit)

The ESP32-S3 sets the servo torque limit; the servo reports its current. Worm friction 0.15 is steel on PA-CF with a little grease.

**Suggested: 0.6 A (torque limit about 17 %).** About 18 N per finger, 1.1 kg held by friction alone with a safety factor of 2 and 5 m/s² of arm acceleration. The Fin Ray fingers also wrap the object, which adds to this.

## Other numbers

- **Worm axial load:** 215 N, pushing the worm **up**, away from the servo. It goes into the F6-12M thrust ball bearing of the palm, not into the servo.
- **Worm efficiency:** 33%. The lead angle is 4.3°, so the worm cannot be back-driven for any friction above 0.075. The grip holds with the power off.
- **Speed:** 45 rpm at the worm, 15°/s at the fingers. Fully open to an 80 mm object: 2.3 s; fully open to tips touching: 4.6 s (3.5 worm turns). Opening only as far as the object needs (the servo knows the position) makes a grasp take 0.3–0.5 s.
- **Power through the coils:** about 7 W while squeezing at this limit, 1–2 W while closing, plus about 1 W for the ESP32-S3 (BLE). Check the link on the bench.
- **Wheel teeth:** 11 MPa on a module 1.5 printed tooth (hand v2.6: 18 MPa on module 1). Print the finger roots flat, 100% infill round the teeth, PA-CF.
- **Servo:** it works at 17% of its stall torque, only while squeezing; the self-locking worm holds the object, so the servo can be switched off once the grip is made.

## Assumptions

- Contact 55 mm from the finger pivot; TPU friction 0.6; the three fingers share the load equally.
- Servo torque proportional to current above the no-load current; stall figures are the maker's.
- A wet or oily object halves the friction: the form closure of the Fin Rays then does most of the work.
