Passive Hand

Passive hand

No microcontroller, no radio and no firmware in the hand. The wrist flange picks the frequency of the inductive carrier: at 98 kHz the hand closes, at 207 kHz it opens, with the carrier off it holds still. Schematics, starting values and a bench test plan.

What the hand does with each command

98 kHz
close

IN1 goes high. When the Fin Ray fingers touch the object the motor stalls, the current rises and the flange turns the bridge off.

207 kHz
open

IN2 goes high. With the fingers fully open the NC limit switch opens, IN2 drops and the motor stops by itself.

off
hold

No power in the hand. The worm drive is self-locking and holds the grip.

Hand schematic

The power path is a plain rectifier. The two filters take their signal from the top coil terminal, which swings between 0 and VM, and only choose the direction.

Why these values

f₀ = 1 / (2π·√(L·C)) 2.2 mH + 1.2 nF → 98 kHz 2.2 mH + 270 pF → 207 kHz Q = √(L/C) / R ≈ 2.9 on both (470 Ω and 1 kΩ) right channel: the coil terminal swings 0…VM → fundamental ≈ 0.64·VM with VM = 10 V: ≈ 6.4 V peak → ≈ 5.8 V after the diode → IN ≈ 2.5 V (needs > 1.5 V) wrong channel: about 21 % gets through → < 2 V after the diode → blocked by the 3.3 V zener maximum force: I_lim ≈ 64 / R_ILIM[kΩ] → 82 kΩ ≈ 0.78 A (check the formula in the datasheet)

The low Q is on purpose: with ±10% tolerance on inductors and capacitors the filter stays centred well enough. The zener does the separating, not the filter selectivity.

Flange schematic

The bridge is not tuned (the series capacitor resonates much lower), so both frequencies pass and the MOSFETs switch at zero voltage. The shunt sits under local ground, so it measures the current drawn from the 24 V.

  • MOSFETs: N-channel, at least 40 V (60 V is better), RDS(on) ≤ 50 mΩ at 10 V, Qg ≤ 20 nC.
  • No ready-made "ZVS" module: it oscillates at its own frequency and cannot be commanded.
  • XIAO: three pins (PWM, enable, ADC). The INA180 runs from the XIAO's 3.3 V. The cable to the camera goes from 2 to 5 wires (5 V, ground, PWM, enable, current), in the same flange groove.
  • IR2104 input threshold: about 3 V, so the XIAO's 3.3 V is enough but close to the limit: check the datasheet of the part you buy.

What the XIAO firmware does

open() 207 kHz until the current drops to the idle level (limit switch) → finger zero close() 98 kHz until the current stays above the stall threshold for 30 ms → bridge off open_to(θ) open(), then close for a time t = k·θ calibrated once speed 2–5 kHz bursts with variable duty (the DRV sees a PWM on its inputs) hold bridge off: the worm holds safety 4 s timeout on every move; the emergency button cuts the 24 V and everything stops

Does the worm hold?

Yes, as long as it is not greased. Module 2, 16 mm pitch diameter, single start: the lead angle is atan(2/16) = 7.1°. A worm is self-locking when the friction angle atan(µ / cos 20°) is larger than the lead angle, that is for µ above about 0.12.

Friction µConditionFriction angleResult
0.08well-greased PETG4.9°back-drives

At most a film of dry PTFE. On top of that, the motor gearbox already brakes a lot by itself. The price is efficiency: about 36% forwards, so the motor works a bit harder to squeeze.

What changes in the model

  • Hand v2.3: the ESP32-C3 and the power module are gone. In their place, a single 20 × 25 mm board (rectifier, DRV8871, two filters) in the 0° rail. The 47 µF capacitor keeps its seat; the 240° rail is no longer needed. Hand height and grip position are unchanged.
  • Flange: the TX driver becomes the bridge with shunt and current sensor, in the same 30 × 20 × 9 mm envelope.
